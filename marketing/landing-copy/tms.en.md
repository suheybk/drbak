# TMS — Transcranial Magnetic Stimulation

**Magnetic pulses targeting specific cortical regions; an evidence-based,
drug-free treatment option.**

## What is TMS?

TMS (Transcranial Magnetic Stimulation) is a **non-invasive** brain
stimulation method. Brief magnetic pulses are delivered through a coil
placed against the scalp; these pulses induce small electrical currents in
the cortical neurons beneath the coil, which in turn modulate synaptic
plasticity (the brain's capacity to change).

It is used for patients who don't respond to, or can't tolerate,
conventional medication, either as a stand-alone treatment or in addition
to one. The U.S. FDA has cleared TMS for depression (2008), OCD (2018) and
smoking cessation (2020); CE-marked devices in Europe are used across a
wider range of indications.

## Who it's for

TMS is evaluated by a sub-specialist physician for:

- **Treatment-resistant depression** — major depression that hasn't
  responded to at least two adequate trials of antidepressant medication.
- **Anxiety disorders** — especially when accompanying depression.
- **Obsessive-compulsive disorder** — in addition to or as an alternative
  to medication and/or psychotherapy.
- **Migraine** — as preventive therapy for chronic or episodic migraine.
- **Tinnitus** — particularly forms with a neurological component.
- **Parkinson's disease** — as supportive motor-symptom management.
- **Post-stroke rehabilitation** — protocols supporting motor and language
  recovery.

Other indications (PTSD, ADHD, bipolar disorder, neuropathic pain, restless
legs syndrome, autism spectrum) sit at varying levels of evidence; in
those cases an individualised risk-benefit analysis is performed.

## Who it isn't for

This part matters; we'll be direct:

- **Ferromagnetic metal in the head** (e.g. aneurysm clips) — absolute
  contraindication.
- **Cochlear implant** — depends on implant type and clinical assessment.
- **Cardiac pacemaker or implanted neurostimulator** — requires risk
  assessment.
- **History of seizures or epilepsy** — risk is elevated; protocol is
  adapted, or TMS is not recommended.
- **Medication pump** — assessed case by case.
- **Pregnancy** — evidence is limited; we avoid unnecessary use.

A pre-screening form is completed from your account (`/account` → TMS
pre-screening) before the first appointment. Pre-screening is mandatory
before TMS and is valid for 365 days.

## What a session feels like

Each session lasts 20–40 minutes. A typical course runs 4–6 weeks at five
sessions per week.

- You sit in a chair; eyes can stay open.
- The coil rests against a specific spot on your scalp; nothing is
  attached.
- The magnetic pulses produce a "click" sound — headphones are provided.
- A mild tingling or muscle twitch on the scalp is possible; it's not
  painful.
- Daily activities, including driving, can resume immediately afterward.

The first sessions calibrate the target spot, so the first session may run
slightly longer.

## Preparation and aftercare

**Before:** keep your caffeine intake at your normal level; sleep matters;
bring your medication list. Hair products (gel, spray) can interfere with
coil contact.

**After:** the most common side-effects are mild headache or fatigue,
which resolve within a few hours. Seizure has been reported very rarely;
this is why personal history of lowered seizure threshold is screened
carefully.

## Evidence

For depression, TMS has been studied in many randomised controlled trials;
meta-analyses show added benefit after medication non-response (e.g.
*George et al., 2010*; *Berlim et al., 2014*). For OCD, a deep-TMS
protocol received FDA clearance in 2018. Evidence continues to accumulate
in migraine and other indications, so we manage expectations carefully and
discuss the rationale for the chosen protocol.

> **In one sentence:** TMS is "worth trying, but not a guarantee." A plan
> is built by reading it together with your history.

## FAQ

**Is TMS painful?**
No. A mild tingling or muscle twitch may occur; patients don't report it
as pain.

**How many sessions are needed?**
Typically 5 sessions/week for 4–6 weeks. Shorter protocols (e.g.
theta-burst) apply in some indications.

**Will I see results immediately?**
Most patients notice change within 2–3 weeks. Full response is assessed
after the course completes.

**Is TMS the same as ECT?**
No. ECT is performed under general anaesthesia and induces seizures
deliberately; TMS is performed awake and does not target seizures.

**Can I drive after a session?**
Yes.
