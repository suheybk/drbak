What is Epilepsy (Seizure Disorder)?
Specialist Dr. Oğuz BAK > Blog > Our Services > What is Epilepsy (Seizure Disorder)?

Epilepsy is a neurological disorder caused by sudden and uncontrolled electrical discharges in brain cells. These electrical activities can cause temporary changes in consciousness, muscle contractions, emotional fluctuations, or short-term behavioral disturbances called "seizures." Not all epileptic seizures are the same; some patients experience only a few seconds of absentmindedness, while others may experience full-body convulsions.
Causes of Epilepsy

Epilepsy is a disease that can arise from various causes.
Some cases are congenital, while others develop later in life. The most common causes are:

    Genetic predisposition: A family history of epilepsy increases the risk.
    Cerebrovascular diseases: These can occur after strokes or brain hemorrhages.
    Trauma: Head trauma can create epileptic foci.
    Infections: These can occur after brain infections such as meningitis or encephalitis.
    Tumors and structural brain abnormalities are also among the important causes of epilepsy.

Symptoms of Epilepsy

Epileptic seizures vary from person to person.
However, the most common symptoms are:

    Loss of consciousness or feeling of fainting.
    Convulsion, trembling, or falling
    Brief pauses in speech or movement
    Repetitive hand, arm, or facial movements
    Post-seizure fatigue, headache, feeling of confusion

In some types of epilepsy, seizures last only seconds and may go unnoticed by those around you.
How is epilepsy diagnosed?

Epilepsy is diagnosed through clinical evaluation, EEG (Electroencephalography) , and brain imaging techniques.
Abnormal electrical activity may be observed on EEG. Imaging methods such as MRI or CT scans can reveal structural causes.

The diagnostic process is completed by obtaining a detailed seizure history from the patient, determining the frequency, duration, and triggering factors of the seizures.
Epilepsy Treatment

The goal of epilepsy treatment is to stop seizures completely or to minimize them.
The treatment plan is determined based on the patient's seizure type, age, underlying cause, and lifestyle.

Treatment methods:

    Medication: The vast majority of people with epilepsy can live a seizure-free life with regular medication.
    Surgical treatment: In cases resistant to drug treatment, the epileptic focus can be surgically removed.
    Neural stimulation methods: Vagal nerve stimulation or deep brain stimulation may be effective in some patients.
    Lifestyle modifications: Getting regular sleep, avoiding alcohol and stimulants, and not interrupting medication increase treatment success.

Epilepsy and Daily Life

With the right treatment, people with epilepsy can lead completely normal lives .
Having accurate information about the disease, taking medication regularly, and having regular doctor check-ups are of great importance.

For individuals diagnosed with epilepsy, living in a safe and supportive environment free from societal prejudice strengthens both their psychological and physical recovery process.

Neuropathic Pain Treatment

What is neuropathic pain?

Neuropathic pain is a type of pain that arises from damage or dysfunction in the nervous system. This condition often manifests as burning, tingling, stabbing, or electric shock sensations. Conventional painkillers are generally ineffective for this type of pain because the problem originates directly from nerve cells.
Treatment Goal and Approach

The primary goal in treating neuropathic pain is to identify and control the cause of the pain.
For example, in diabetic patients, high blood sugar levels can impair nerve nourishment, leading to neuropathic pain. In this case, treatment should begin with stabilizing blood sugar levels.
Restoring healthy nerve nourishment helps both reduce pain and prevent its progression.
Drug and Interventional Treatment Options

Treatment for neuropathic pain is multifaceted.
Classic analgesics are often insufficient, so specialized drug groups and interventional methods are preferred.
The following medications and methods may be used in treatment:

    Special antiepileptic drugs that affect the nervous system
    Rhythm-regulating (antiarrhythmic) drugs
    Muscle relaxants
    Morphine-derived drugs
    Local anesthetic applications

In patients who do not respond to drug therapy or for whom drug use is unsuitable, interventional nerve block techniques can be an effective alternative. These procedures reduce pain by temporarily halting nerve conduction.
Supportive Approach with Ozone Therapy

In some patients, multipass ozone therapy can be applied in addition to conventional treatments .
Ozone supports the nourishment of nerve tissue by increasing oxygenation at the cellular level and reduces inflammation. This can help reduce neuropathic pain and significantly decrease the patient's need for medication.
Conclusion

Neuropathic pain treatment is not a single approach; it is a multifaceted process tailored to the individual based on the underlying cause.
With the selection of appropriate medication, along with supportive methods such as interventional nerve blocks and ozone therapy when necessary, the quality of life can be significantly improved for many patients.

What is Restless Legs Syndrome? Symptoms and Treatment Methods
Specialist Dr. Oğuz BAK > Blog > Our Services > What is Restless Legs Syndrome? Symptoms and Treatment Methods

Restless Legs Syndrome (RLS) is a neurological disorder characterized by discomfort, tingling, burning, pulling, or an urge to move the legs, especially at night .
It makes it difficult to fall asleep, causes frequent awakenings, and reduces quality of life in the long term.
🧠 Causes of Restless Legs Syndrome

While the exact cause of HBS is not always known, dopamine imbalance or iron deficiency often plays a significant role.
Additionally, the following conditions can increase the risk:

    Prolonged periods of immobility or working night shifts,
    Pregnancy,
    Kidney diseases,
    Neuropathy and the use of certain medications,
    Genetic predisposition.

⚕️ Diagnosis and Assessment Process

Neurological examinations and sleep tests (PSG) performed by Specialist Dr. Oğuz Bak determine the severity and possible causes of the disorder.
In some cases , EEG or EMG tests are also included in the process to differentiate the symptoms from other neurological diseases.
💊 Treatment Approach

The goal of treating Restless Legs Syndrome is to reduce symptoms and improve sleep quality.
Treatment plans are tailored to the individual and may include the following methods:

    Dopamine agonists or iron supplements,
    Lifestyle changes that improve sleep hygiene,
    Ozone or glutathione supplementation therapies may be used when needed .
    Neural therapy or supportive treatments aimed at restoring vitamin and mineral balance .
